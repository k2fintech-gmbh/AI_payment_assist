# Jurisdictions snapshot

Snapshot date: 2026-10-20. Lists change several times a year. Always verify the current version live (see `open_sources.md`) and tell the user which version you relied on. If live verification fails, use this snapshot and say so explicitly, naming the snapshot date.

## 1. FATF — High-Risk Jurisdictions subject to a Call for Action ("black list")

Version: FATF statement of 19 June 2026. Source: https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/countries/black-and-grey-lists.html

- Democratic People's Republic of Korea (North Korea)
- Iran
- Myanmar

FATF calls for enhanced due diligence and, for DPRK and Iran, counter-measures. Payments involving these countries are very likely to be rejected by EU banks.

FATF does not call for enhanced due diligence for grey-list countries, but banks take the listing into account in their risk analysis. Note that Bulgaria and Monaco are on the grey list although Bulgaria is an EU member state and both use the euro; SEPA payments still work, but banks may ask more questions.

EU banks must apply enhanced due diligence to business relationships and transactions involving these countries (Article 18a of Directive (EU) 2015/849). In practice: more questions, documents on purpose and source of funds, longer processing, and some banks decline such payments altogether.

The EU list lags behind FATF changes: a country removed from the FATF grey list (e.g. Algeria, Namibia in June 2026) may remain on the EU list until the Commission amends it.

## 4. Countries with broad EU restrictive measures (non-exhaustive)

Always check the EU Sanctions Map for the current scope: https://www.sanctionsmap.eu

- **Russia**: extensive sectoral sanctions, transaction bans with many banks and state entities, SWIFT disconnection of designated banks, import/export bans, restrictions on deposits and crypto services. Many EU banks have stopped payments to and from Russia altogether.
- **Belarus**: sectoral sanctions closely aligned with Russia measures, including SWIFT disconnection of designated banks.
- **Iran**: asset freezes, trade restrictions; on the FATF black list.
- **North Korea (DPRK)**: near-total restrictions; on the FATF black list.
- **Syria**: the EU lifted most economic sanctions in 2025 but kept listings of individuals and some sectors; banks remain very cautious.
- Other regimes exist (e.g. Afghanistan/Taliban, Libya, Myanmar, Venezuela, Sudan, Mali, territories of Ukraine not controlled by the government: Crimea/Sevastopol, Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia, Kherson). Check the map for the specific country.

## 5. Practical consequences to explain to the user

| Situation | What usually happens |
| - | - |
| Black-list country | Payment will very likely be refused; do not plan around it. |
| Comprehensive or broad EU sanctions | Payment may be prohibited; only licensed or exempted cases (e.g. some humanitarian, food, medicine) are possible, and only through the bank's own process. |
| EU high-risk list or FATF grey list | Enhanced due diligence: expect questions about purpose, relationship and source of funds; prepare documents; allow extra time. |
| Tax non-cooperative jurisdiction | Extra questions on business rationale; prepare contract and invoice. |
| No listing | Standard checks only. |
