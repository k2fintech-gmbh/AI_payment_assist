# Supporting documents and payment descriptions

## 1. Why banks ask for documents (generic explanation)

EU banks and payment institutions must understand the purpose of a transaction and, for larger or unusual payments, the source of the funds (Directive (EU) 2015/849 and, from July 2027, Regulation (EU) 2024/1624). There is no single EU-wide amount above which documents are always required: each bank sets its own risk-based rules. Documents are more likely to be requested when:

- the amount is large compared to the account's usual activity;
- the recipient or a bank in the chain is in a higher-risk or sanctioned country;
- the purpose is vague, or the payment is to a new recipient;
- the payment involves crypto, investments, high-value goods, real estate or loans;
- the payment is between a business and a private person, or between related parties.

Never state or guess a specific bank's threshold. Never suggest how to avoid a document request.

## 2. Document checklist by purpose

| Purpose | Usually requested | Helpful extras |
| - | - | - |
| Payment for goods (B2B) | Invoice (seller and buyer names matching the payment parties, invoice number and date, goods description, amount, currency, bank details) | Contract or purchase order; proof of delivery or transport documents (bill of lading, CMR, airway bill); customs declaration; for dual-use or controlled goods, export licence or confirmation that none is needed |
| Payment for services | Invoice; service contract | Statement of work, acceptance certificate, correspondence showing the service |
| Salary / contractor fees | Employment or service contract | Payslip; invoice from contractor |
| Rent | Lease agreement | Landlord's identity matching account holder |
| Real estate purchase or deposit | Preliminary or final purchase agreement; notary or lawyer details | Proof of source of funds (sale of previous property, savings statements, mortgage approval); escrow / notary account confirmation |
| Loan to a person or company | Signed loan agreement (amount, interest, repayment schedule) | Source of funds; relationship explanation |
| Loan repayment | Original loan agreement; repayment schedule | Previous transfers showing the loan disbursement |
| Gift or family support | Short explanation of relationship | Proof of relationship (if asked); source of funds for large gifts |
| Tuition / education | Invoice or enrolment letter from the institution | Student's name matching the invoice |
| Medical treatment abroad | Invoice or cost estimate from the clinic | Clinic's registration |
| Investment / securities | Contract with an authorised firm; account opening confirmation | Regulator register entry for the firm |
| Crypto purchase via exchange | Account confirmation with the exchange (name matches) | Exchange's MiCA authorisation (EU) |
| Charity / donation | Charity's registration details | Receipt |
| Inheritance distribution | Certificate of inheritance or court / notary decision | Estate account details |
| Dividends | Shareholder resolution, dividend statement | Company accounts |
| Return of funds / refund | Original payment record; reason | Correspondence |

## 3. Source of funds (when asked)

Typical evidence: salary slips, bank statements, sale agreement for an asset (car, property, shares), inheritance documents, loan agreement, company dividends, tax return. The documents should explain where the money on the account came from, not only where it is going.

## 4. Document quality checklist

- Names of payer and payee on the document match the account holders exactly.
- Amount and currency on the document match (or explain the difference, e.g. partial payment, advance).
- The document is dated, numbered and complete; signed where a signature is expected.
- Bank details on an invoice match the IBAN the user intends to pay. If the bank details changed recently, verify by phone using a number from an independent source (invoice-redirection fraud).
- Documents in uncommon languages may need a translation.

## 5. Writing the payment description

Good descriptions state what the payment is for, in plain terms, using real references:

- "Invoice 4521 dated 03.03.2026 – consulting services March 2026"
- "Rent April 2026, flat 12, Via Roma 5, Milan"
- "Gift to my daughter Anna for her wedding"
- "Repayment of loan agreement of 15.01.2026, instalment 3 of 12"
- "Tuition fee spring semester 2026, student ID [if needed]"

Avoid vague words on their own: "payment", "services", "transfer", "personal", "goods", "help", "loan" (without agreement reference).

Rules:

1. The description must reflect the purpose the user actually stated.
2. Never propose wording intended to reduce the chance of review.
3. If a supporting document is attached, the description may use its invoice number and date only if they are visible in the document; otherwise ask the user.
4. SEPA allows 140 characters; SWIFT payment details allow similar length. Keep it short and factual.
